# Setting up a channel, the first week, mistakes

## Setup, in order

1. A Google account used only for this business; **2-step verification on** immediately (needed
   for monetization; a hijacked channel loses everything).
2. Create the channel as a **Brand Account** so managers and editors get access without the
   password.
3. Name and handle: easy to say aloud and spell after hearing once; not close to a bigger channel.
4. Profile picture and banner (Canva is fine), readable at tiny sizes.
5. About and description for humans: who it's for, what they get, how often. A few keywords in
   natural sentences.
6. **Verify** with your own phone number (never rented or temporary - you'll need codes again).
   Custom thumbnails and long uploads depend on it; Studio's Feature eligibility page shows what's
   locked.
7. Upload defaults in Studio: category, language, audience. Most faceless channels are "not made
   for kids".

## Warming up (no magic)

New accounts that upload a pile of videos within minutes look like automation. So: use the account
like a human for a week or two (watch the niche, subscribe, real comments); finish profile and
verification before uploading; start at one or two videos a week; no ten uploads on day one; don't
keep renaming the channel.

Myths: no warm-up routine guarantees views; "trust score" is not a number you can see; proxies and
anti-detect browsers don't make a channel succeed and evading enforcement is what gets account
networks removed together. **Don't build a business that needs to hide from YouTube.** One AdSense
account per person; never a relative's identity.

## Account structure

One Google account can hold several channels (Brand Accounts). Existing account history helps.
Multiple channels make sense only when expanding from a proven one, or for a 2-3 niche fishnet
test over a couple of months. Editors get channel permissions, never the password.

## First week

1. Study 5 channels; fill 10 spreadsheet rows.
2. Demand check on three niches; fill the scorecard.
3. Google account, Brand Account channel, profile, verification.
4. Persona sheet or voice choice; build the JSON template.
5. Research and write the first brief and script.
6. Record voice, gather visuals, rough cut.
7. Finish, thumbnail, AI disclosure if needed, publish. Then start the email lead magnet.

## Roadmap

Study channels -> patterns -> demand gap -> scorecard niche -> build channel -> research topics ->
verified briefs -> structured scripts -> voice -> visuals -> edit for retention -> package ->
publish consistently -> analytics -> improve format -> raise RPM -> affiliates -> email list ->
digital products -> first hires -> document systems -> replicate -> more channels -> operator.

## Mistakes that sink new channels

Ten near-identical videos in a week; trusting AI facts; copying footage or scripts; chasing
proxies, tools and "trust score" before making a good video; ignoring the first 30 seconds;
quitting after twelve videos; AdSense only; fake reviews, testimonials or customers; sharing
passwords with editors; never checking the retention graph.

## FAQ

- Faceless channels still monetizable in 2026? Yes; mass-produced repetitive content is what isn't.
- Disclose AI? Realistic content that could be mistaken for real: yes. Scripts, ideas, captions:
  no. Unsure: disclose.
- Monetized before February? Apply now if you meet 1,000 subs + 4,000 h or 10M Shorts views.
- Nowhere near? Build for the new bar and start email, affiliates and a small product.
- Buy a channel or a course? Almost never.

## Glossary

RPM revenue per 1,000 views after YouTube's cut. CPM advertiser cost per 1,000 impressions before
the cut. CTR share of impressions that clicked. AVD average view duration. Retention the graph of
who is still watching. Impressions times the thumbnail was shown. YPP YouTube Partner Program.
Outlier a video far above its channel's normal. Swipe file saved examples. EPC earnings per
affiliate click. CMS back-end system for networks and rights holders.
