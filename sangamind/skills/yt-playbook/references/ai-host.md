# AI character hosts

Faceless channels hit a wall around video forty: people subscribe to someone, not a topic. A
consistent host - face, voice, personality - solves attachment without the creator on camera. Done
badly it is an uncanny talking head grouped with the slop.

## Guardrails

- Photorealistic avatars are auto-labelled; turn disclosure on yourself. The label costs nothing.
- **Never resemble a real person** - celebrity, creator, anyone. Change face, hair, age, outfit
  until it is nobody.
- **Expertise:** an AI host on health or money implies authority. Educate, cite sources, never
  claim credentials; have a qualified human review medical or financial scripts.
- The avatar is the host; research, angle and storytelling are the show. An avatar reading
  near-identical scripts is exactly "inauthentic content".
- About section: "Our host is an AI character." Transparency builds trust.
- **Must read as an adult.** A childlike character risks "made for kids" classification (no
  personalised ads, no comments) and attracts the wrong audience.
- **No ethnicity-as-the-joke.** Ground the character's skill in her story, not a stereotype.

## What works

Consistent look, voice and personality; a point of view, running jokes, a signature opening or
closing habit; host mixed with strong visuals, not 12 minutes of talking head; short, sharp
scripts (an avatar exposes weak writing); host does intros, reactions, transitions, verdicts.

Example: Juno, dry and sceptical gadget host, ends every video ranking products "from least to most
likely to end up in a drawer". Never claims to have used anything: "I've read 400 reviews of these
so you don't have to."

## Building one

1. **Persona sheet** - name, age range, personality, how they talk, what they care about, three
   things they'd never say, one catchphrase or habit. Short, fictional backstory.
2. **Look** - image model (ChatGPT images, Midjourney, Nano Banana...). Borrow a niche's energy,
   then change everything identifiable.
3. **Character sheet** - plain background: front, side, three-quarter, full body; expressions
   neutral, amused, serious, surprised. Every later image references it. Consistency is the trick.
4. **Location and style sheets** - each place the host lives from a few angles; one style sheet
   (lighting, palette, camera feel). Decide once, reuse.
5. **Voice** - fits the niche; clone only your own voice with consent, or hire a voice actor. Read
   commercial-use terms before publishing.
6. **Animate** - lip-sync avatar tool for talking segments, video model for scenes. Prompt formula:
   character from sheet + location from sheet + specific action + camera angle and movement +
   light direction and mood + style from sheet. "Girl talking in her room" is why AI video looks
   bad.
7. **Write in persona** - the JSON script template with the persona sheet as a rule.
8. **Assemble** - avatar segments with maps, charts, footage, graphics; captions; cut dead air;
   disclose.
9. **Review like a stranger** - first 60 s on a phone, sound off then on. Would you subscribe?

Stylised (anime, chibi-adult, 3D cartoon) is cheaper, avoids the uncanny valley and the slop
pop-up, and holds consistency better than photoreal. Month-1 budget option: still poses from the
character sheet with motion, voice and captions; full lip-sync once there is income.

Hybrid: a hired human host or voice actor also solves the human-signal problem. Test it against
the AI voice early and let the retention graph decide.
