# Researching what works, without copying

Copying = taking a script, thumbnail, edit or concept and recreating it with nothing of yours.
Studying = working out WHY it worked and applying the reason your own way. Every successful video
is a free public case study.

## Break a video into parts (watch like a mechanic)

topic, title, thumbnail, hook (first 5-30 s), first 30 s, story structure, length, editing pace,
voice, music, visuals, pattern interrupts, retention moments (re-hooks), call to action, comments
(especially repeated questions), channel upload frequency. One spreadsheet row per video.

Ask every time: why this thumbnail, why this title over the obvious one, why open this way, why
reveal that fact at that moment, why this length, why it beat the channel's average, where the
pacing changes, what made people comment, **what made people watch to the end** (usually: it kept
making a promise and partly keeping it).

## Pick 5-10 channels on purpose

- 2-3 giants - the ceiling and the polish.
- 3 at your size or just ahead - tactics you can use this month.
- 1-2 with a recent breakout (e.g. stuck at 8k for a year, then 80k in four months) - find what
  changed right before: titles, thumbnails, narrower niche, new format, a series.
- 1-2 started in the last six months - what works now with no legacy audience.

For each: last 20 uploads -> outliers, average, upload rhythm, what they quietly stopped (an
abandoned series probably didn't pay). One creator doing something is taste; five doing it is
strategy. Example: all five caption from second one -> sound-off viewers matter.

## Study the system, not only the video

When a whole channel outperforms (every upload far above its subscriber count), the secret is usually
the **series system**, not any one video: one premise, one title formula, one thumbnail system,
light lore, everyday subjects. Example, studied 23 Oct 2026: Bradikinin (faceless, pictograms, calm
narrator) - "If Your Phone Ever Charges To 101%… DO NOT UNPLUG IT" 1.6M, eight videos in 11 days all
126K-2.6M at 41.8K subscribers; the hook counts the parts ("seven rules; most people break the
third"). Copy the *system idea*, never its premise or its look (creator-package, "The series system").

## Outliers

A video far above its channel's normal (20k average, one at 2M = 100x). `/creator-viral` ranks these.
Investigate in order: topic (big in general or to this audience?), timing (news, release, trend),
thumbnail, title (curiosity gap), format, hook, audience, search demand, external events.

Topic or packaging? (1) Other channels covered the same topic and flopped -> topic isn't the secret.
(2) Same packaging on different topics also worked -> packaging is the secret. Compare each winner
with the channel's failure right next to it.

## Swipe file

From day one: thumbnails that stopped the scroll, titles that got the click, hooks, story
structures, transitions, revealing comments, every outlier. Tag niche + one line "why I think this
worked".

## Gaps (demand beats supply)

Topics wanted but uncovered; popular topics with poor videos; audiences not served in their
language or level; slow-uploading channels; weak thumbnails; flat storytelling; **formats that
win in one niche and haven't reached another**.

**Format transfer** - ask "where else could this work?": documentary storytelling -> sports seasons;
podcast-fast editing -> history; celebrity-news rhythm -> internet personalities; mystery case-file
-> company collapses; long documentary -> one game's development hell.

## Ten-minute demand check

1. Search the topic; count good results from the last 90 days.
2. Views vs channel size - small channels with big views = hungry audience.
3. Search-bar autocomplete = real searches.
4. Google Trends, 12 months: rising, flat, dying.
5. Comments on the top three: what did people ask that the video didn't deliver?

Old, thin or badly made results with healthy views = a lane. A recent, definitive video in the
millions = the story is owned; pass unless the topic qualifies as a proven-topic slot (yt-strategy,
"Topics").

## Where to look

YouTube search filtered to this week/month sorted by views; each channel's Videos tab by Popular;
Google Trends; Reddit and forums; comment sections. vidIQ, TubeBuddy and outlier finders help but
change constantly - learn the manual method first.

**Study more than you publish.** A hundred videos repeating one mistake is one video of experience.
Spend week one studying, week two making.

## Add your own value

Better research, storytelling, commentary, visuals or pacing; a different audience, language or
level; more context; a unique perspective. Take a proven concept and execute it better, or aim it
at someone nobody serves.
